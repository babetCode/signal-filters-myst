---
title: Philisophical Glossary
---

:::{glossary}
Abstraction  
: The process of removing specific details to create a generalized concept or model from concrete examples (also a noun to refer to these models).

Conciousness  
The presence of qualia.

Conclusion  
: A statement that is supported by one or more other statements in an argument.

Deductive Reasoning  
: Reasoning in which the premises are intended to make the conclusion necessarily true: if the premises are true, the conclusion cannot be false.


Physical World**</u>  
A conceptual model containing notions of space, time, and their contents (or a unified space-time in more refined models). Since space and time (or space-time) are the foundations of the model, but are many layers of abstraction away from qualia, physical world models do not contain notions of qualia or consciousness.

Premise  
: A statement offered as a reason for accepting another statement.

Quale  
: An instance of subjective experience, such as a color or sensation (plural: qualia).

Statement  
: A declarative sentence that's capable of being true or false.
:::
